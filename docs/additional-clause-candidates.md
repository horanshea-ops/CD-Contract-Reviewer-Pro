# Candidate clause categories beyond the v1 list

Not built out. Flagged in [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md) pending SME availability.

The build brief §1 names 11 clause types, all seeded in the v1 standards library.
Beyond those, standard hospitality-contracting practice commonly treats the
following as separate negotiated points, each capable of creating financial or
operational exposure. None of these have industry-default language drafted yet —
listing them here is scoping, not a recommendation to build all of them.

- **Insurance & indemnification.** Whether the group is required to carry liability
  insurance / name the hotel as additional insured, and how broadly indemnification
  language shifts risk (e.g. indemnifying the hotel for its own negligence, which is
  a real exposure point in poorly drafted contracts).
- **Damage / security deposit.** Deposit terms, what counts as damage, and timelines
  for return — distinct from the master account billing terms already covered.
- **Exclusivity / outside vendor restrictions.** Mandatory use of in-house AV,
  catering, or decor vendors, and the fees attached to bringing in outside vendors.
- **Termination for convenience vs. termination for cause.** Whether either party can
  exit early without triggering the cancellation damages clause, and under what
  conditions.
- **Assignment / subcontracting.** Whether the group can transfer the contract to
  another entity (relevant for associations that rotate management companies).
- **Brand or ownership change.** What happens if the property changes flag,
  management company, or ownership between signing and the event — a real
  operational risk industry-wide, distinct from construction/renovation.
- **Named-storm / hurricane-specific language.** In coastal and hurricane-belt
  markets, force majeure is often supplemented with a separate, narrower
  "hurricane clause" with its own notice and cancellation-window terms — worth
  treating separately from general force majeure once segment data exists.
- **Attendee/guest data handling.** Whether the hotel can use rooming list or
  attendee data for its own marketing — increasingly a negotiated point, and
  adjacent to (but distinct from) the client-confidentiality question already
  tracked in the build brief.

## Why these are parked, not built

Two things the build brief is explicit about (§7) can't come from published
industry material: the tacit trades (what CD concedes first) and severity
calibration (what counts as high vs. a note). Adding these categories with
generic industry-default language would just be more stage-1 scaffolding — more
surface area to relabel once real CD contracts and senior-associate input are
available, without adding real accuracy in the meantime. Revisit this list
alongside stage-2/3 library work once an SME is validating entries.
